# Firing Schedule

## Build the Queue

Submit work by the studio cutoff with the maker's name, clay body, firing type, target cone, piece count, and contact details. Confirm that each piece is bone dry, identified on its underside, and within the posted size and weight limits. Group compatible work by program; do not combine loads merely to fill empty space when temperatures or glaze risks differ.

## Choose a Program

The firing lead reviews the queue against kiln capacity, shelf space, element condition, and upcoming events. Use the approved bisque or glaze program rather than changing ramps casually. Add a slower schedule for thick sections only when the lead has assessed the load. Flag low-fire, mid-fire, test, and uncertain work clearly so a mistaken program is less likely.

## Communicate Results

Post the planned kiln number, load date, program, and expected unload date. If work is delayed, cracked, stuck, or held for inspection, tell the maker and update the queue instead of guessing. After unloading, record actual peak temperature, hold time, witness-cone results, and any abnormal alarms. Retain failed test pieces for troubleshooting when space permits.
