# Dust Control

## Prevent Dust

Keep powdered clay, glaze, and dry sanding to the designated area, with local ventilation running. Wear the respiratory protection required by the studio risk assessment when handling powders. Avoid shaking dusty aprons, sweeping dry floors, using compressed air, or blowing across shelves. Store powders closed and clean spills before they are tracked through the room.

## Clean Without Spreading

Use a damp sponge, wet mop, or studio-approved HEPA vacuum for settled material. Work from clean surfaces toward the dirtiest area, and rinse cleaning water into the solids-collection system rather than a regular drain. Clean shelves and wheel splash pans before residue dries. Wash hands and forearms after cleanup, even when gloves were worn, and keep food and drinks outside the workroom.

## Respond to Exposure

Stop the activity if a cloud forms or ventilation fails. Move people away from the source, close the container if safe, and notify the studio lead. Anyone with breathing irritation should leave the area and follow the posted first-aid or medical guidance. Record the material, location, and cleanup steps so the next shift can correct the cause.
