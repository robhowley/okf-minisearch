# Bisque Inspection

## Check Surfaces

Handle each piece over a padded table and inspect the foot, rim, attachments, and inside corners. Look for cracks, sharp flakes, kiln wash stuck to the base, loose shelves, pinholes, and pieces fused to neighbors. Run a clean fingertip around the rim; it should not catch on a burr. Brush away loose crumbs outdoors or with the approved damp method, not by blowing.

## Sort for Glaze

Separate sound ware from pieces needing repair, testing, or disposal. Confirm that the clay body and firing temperature match the glaze plan. Wipe the foot with a slightly damp sponge and allow it to dry before applying glaze. If a piece is unusually dark, soft, smoky, or much heavier than expected, hold it for the firing lead because it may not have been fully fired or cooled correctly.

## Record Problems

Mark the maker's work clearly and photograph defects that are easy to miss. Record the piece ID, defect, shelf position, and recommended action on the inspection sheet. Never hide a crack beneath glaze or send questionable ware into a shared firing without the maker's informed decision and lead approval.
