# Pollinator Habitat

A pollinator area gives bees, butterflies, and beneficial insects food and shelter while supporting garden crops. Plan a continuous sequence of blooms from spring through fall, and choose plants that suit the site instead of buying a decorative mix that may not thrive. Keep flowering plants separate from crops that need frequent spraying or digging.

## Choose and Arrange Plants

Combine early flowers such as willow, crocus, or native spring perennials with summer herbs, sunflowers, and fall asters. Cluster each species in patches so insects can find it easily. Include plants with different flower shapes and leave some stems hollow or pithy for nesting. Provide a shallow dish of stones and water, refreshed often, rather than a deep open container.

## Tend the Habitat

Avoid pesticides in the pollinator bed, especially when flowers are open. Hand-pull weeds before they seed, but leave a few non-invasive host plants when they support caterpillars. Do not deadhead every plant in autumn; seed heads feed birds and standing stems shelter insects. Cut old growth in late winter after temperatures rise, and compost only material that is free of disease.

## Share the Space

Post a small sign naming bloom periods and asking visitors not to disturb nesting areas. A monthly count of visiting insects can guide future plant choices and help children participate in stewardship.
