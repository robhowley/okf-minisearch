# Seasonal Planning

Seasonal planning turns a shared garden’s beds, tools, and volunteer time into a manageable crop calendar. Begin with the harvests members want, then fit varieties to the site, last-frost estimate, water capacity, and labor. Leave room for weather delays instead of treating every date as fixed.

## Map the Year

Draw the beds and note sun, shade, paths, irrigation zones, and perennial plants. For each crop, record days to maturity, preferred temperature, spacing, and whether it follows or precedes another crop. Schedule cool-season greens and peas before warm-season tomatoes, peppers, or beans. Reserve at least one bed for flowers, cover crops, or a late-season community planting.

## Stagger and Share

Sow small batches of quick crops every two or three weeks rather than planting the whole bed at once. Staggering harvests prevents a glut and spreads watering and picking tasks. Post the plan at the shed, identify a bed steward, and mark who will start, transplant, weed, and harvest each crop. Use a shared calendar for workdays and irrigation changes.

## Review the Plan

At the end of each season, compare intended and actual harvest dates, note varieties that struggled, and record empty periods or overfilled beds. Carry useful observations into the next calendar, but change one or two variables at a time so the garden learns what actually improved.
