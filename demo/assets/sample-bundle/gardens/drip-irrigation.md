# Drip Irrigation

Drip irrigation delivers water at the soil surface, helping beds use less water while keeping leaves dry. Design each line for the crop spacing and bed length, and label valves so volunteers can shut down one section without guessing. Check the system before the first hot week rather than waiting for wilted plants.

## Set Up the Lines

Start with a filter and pressure regulator if the supply requires them. Lay emitters beside plant roots, not across paths, and secure tubing with pins so it stays in place during weeding. Use a flush end on each line. Separate containers, seedlings, and established crops into zones.

## Water by Observation

Run the system long enough to wet the root zone, then let the surface dry slightly before watering again. Push a finger or trowel several inches into the soil to check moisture; a timer cannot account for rain, mulch, or soil type. Seedlings may need short, frequent cycles, while deep-rooted crops benefit from less frequent soaking.

## Maintain Shared Equipment

Inspect for leaks, clogged emitters, and damaged tubing every week. Flush lines at the ends, clean the filter, and repair punctures with connectors. Drain or remove exposed equipment before freezing weather. Record repairs and the normal schedule on the valve box so the next volunteer can continue without resetting the whole system.
