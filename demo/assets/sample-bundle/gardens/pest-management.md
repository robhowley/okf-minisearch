# Pest Management

Pest management in a community garden starts with prevention and observation, not automatic spraying. A few holes or aphids may be tolerable when plants are growing well. Set an action threshold for each crop, protect young plants, and record what was found so neighboring plot holders can respond consistently.

## Scout Systematically

Walk every bed weekly, checking leaf undersides, stems, new growth, and soil around the plant. Look for chewed edges, stippling, sticky residue, wilt, and clusters of insects. Identify the pest before choosing a treatment; insect damage, nutrient problems, and plant diseases can look similar. Photograph unfamiliar symptoms and ask the garden coordinator before taking action.

## Prevent Problems

Remove diseased leaves promptly and discard them according to the garden’s rules. Use row cover over crops that need protection, keep plants spaced for airflow, and rotate crop families between beds. Water at the soil line and clear fallen fruit that attracts pests. Encourage predators with flowers and avoid broad-spectrum products that harm them.

## Treat Carefully

First try hand-picking, a water spray, pruning, or a barrier. If a product is approved, read its label, protect pollinators, spray only the affected crop, and follow the re-entry and harvest interval. Never apply a neighbor’s unlabelled mixture. Log the date, product, bed, and result so future decisions improve.
