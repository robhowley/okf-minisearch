# Composting

The garden compost system turns plant trimmings and approved kitchen scraps into a soil amendment. A pile needs a mix of moist, nitrogen-rich materials and dry, carbon-rich materials, plus air and enough moisture for microbes to work. Keep the system simple so plot holders can use it correctly.

## What Goes In

Add disease-free leaves, weeds before they set seed, spent annual plants, chopped stems, coffee grounds, and untreated grass clippings. Balance wet greens with dry leaves, shredded cardboard, or straw. Do not add meat, dairy, pet waste, glossy paper, chemically treated wood, or invasive weeds. Put questionable material in the trash or ask the garden coordinator.

## Manage the Pile

Build the pile at least three feet wide, and keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge. Turn or loosen it with a fork when the center is compacted, smells sour, or stays cold after fresh material is added. A covered bin prevents flooding while still allowing airflow. Keep paths clear and close the lid after each deposit.

## Know When It Is Ready

Finished compost is dark, crumbly, and earthy-smelling; the original ingredients are difficult to recognize. Screen out large pieces and return them to the active pile. Spread the finished material around crops or mix a modest amount into new beds rather than burying a thick layer against stems.
