# Seed Starting

Starting seeds indoors lets a community garden grow reliable transplants and spread limited greenhouse space fairly. Match the start date to the crop and the local last-frost estimate, then count backward from the date seedlings should move outside. Keep a log of variety, sowing date, germination, and transplant performance.

## Sow Evenly

Use clean trays or reused pots with drainage holes and a fine, damp seed-starting mix. Plant at the depth on the packet; tiny seeds often need light or only a dusting of mix. Label every container with crop and variety. Cover trays to hold moisture, but uncover them as soon as most seedlings emerge so stems do not stretch or rot.

## Give Seedlings What They Need

Place seedlings under a light close enough to prevent leaning, and run it on a consistent daily schedule. Water from below or with a gentle stream when the surface begins to dry. A small fan moving air across the leaves encourages sturdy stems and lowers disease risk. Thin crowded starts by snipping extras rather than pulling neighboring roots.

## Harden Off

About a week before planting, move trays outdoors for increasing periods in sheltered shade. Gradually introduce sun and wind, and bring them in during cold nights. Transplant after the bed is prepared and the weather matches the crop, then water thoroughly.
