# Harvest and Storage

Storage begins with harvesting at the useful stage and handling produce gently. Pick produce during cool hours, use clean containers, and label donations with the crop, date, and preparation notes. Regular harvests keep plants producing and limit pests.

## Pick for Quality

Harvest leafy greens while leaves are crisp, beans before seeds swell, and tomatoes when they have begun to color if they must travel. Cut squash and herbs with clean tools rather than tearing stems. Keep dry-stored produce unwashed; brush off soil and remove damage. Cool tender crops promptly, but keep onions, garlic, and winter squash dry and warm enough to cure.

## Store by Need

Refrigerate greens, carrots, beets, and most herbs in breathable containers with moisture. Keep potatoes dark and ventilated. Cure onions and garlic in a dry, airy place until skins are papery. Store winter squash where it will not freeze, and inspect it weekly. Keep ethylene-producing ripe fruit away from sensitive greens when possible.

## Preserve and Share

Freeze, dry, ferment, or can surplus only with tested recipes and clean equipment. Mark every jar or bag with contents and date. A harvest board can match extra produce with the community fridge or a scheduled distribution before quality declines. Compost anything unsafe rather than passing it along.
