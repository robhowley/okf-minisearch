# Soil Health

Healthy soil gives the community garden strong roots, steady growth, and fewer irrigation problems. Treat it as a living system rather than an inert planting medium. Observe it each season, add organic matter gradually, and avoid practices that leave beds bare or compacted.

## Check Before Planting

Squeeze a handful of moist soil. It should form a loose ball that crumbles when tapped, not a sticky lump or dusty handful. Look for earthworms, roots, and a range of crumb sizes. A simple soil test can show pH and available nutrients; record results by bed so amendments are based on evidence. Never add lime, sulfur, or fertilizer just because a neighboring plot used it.

## Build Fertility

Spread finished compost in a thin, even layer before planting, then mix it into only the top few inches if the bed needs incorporation. Use cover crops or leaf mulch to feed organisms and reduce erosion. Rotate heavy feeders such as tomatoes with beans or leafy greens, and leave plant residue only when it is healthy.

## Protect the Bed

Keep foot traffic on paths, not growing soil. Use boards or designated stepping stones for shared beds. Mulch exposed ground, and water slowly enough that soil structure stays intact.
