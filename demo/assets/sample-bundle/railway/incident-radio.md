# Incident Radio

## Make the first call
Use the designated emergency channel when there is immediate danger; otherwise use the operating channel. Start with the recipient, your role and location, then say “emergency” or “priority” and wait for acknowledgment when required. State what happened, which line or platform is affected, whether anyone is injured, and whether a train or vehicle is moving. If you do not know a detail, say so instead of guessing.

## Keep instructions clear
The signaller or control lead repeats the protection instruction. Read it back word for word, including signal numbers, line limits, and any prohibition on movement. Use plain verbs such as stop, protect, evacuate, or hold. Do not transmit while another person is giving a protection instruction. If the channel is busy, send the minimum message needed to stop harm and then move to the assigned channel.

## Update and close
Give updates when the situation, access, or casualty status changes. Identify each new message with the incident reference and current location. Control closes the event only after the responsible lead confirms that people are safe, the route is protected or released, and the written incident record is complete.
