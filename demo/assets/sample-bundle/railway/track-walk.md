# Track Walk

## Plan and protect
The patrol leader checks the work order, limits, weather, nearby movements, and safe access point. Obtain formal protection from the signaller and read back the exact track section, line, and release condition. Carry a charged radio, high-visibility clothing, torch, measuring tools, and a way to mark location. Enter only after the signaller confirms that protection is active; keep the escape route visible throughout the walk.

## Inspect systematically
Walk in one direction and call out each reference point, such as a kilometre post, joint, crossing, or bridge. Look for rail cracks, displaced fasteners, broken sleepers, ballast washout, standing water, vegetation, trespass damage, and objects fouling the gauge. Compare unusual geometry with the permitted inspection limits rather than judging by appearance. Photograph or mark a defect only when the work instruction allows it, and never leave a marker that could be mistaken for railway equipment.

## Report and hand back
Give control the location, defect, immediate risk, and recommended restriction in that order. Stop the patrol if weather, visibility, or radio coverage changes. Count tools, clear the line, and obtain explicit release confirmation before normal movements resume.
