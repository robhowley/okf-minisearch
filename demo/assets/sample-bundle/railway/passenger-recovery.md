# Passenger Recovery

## Stabilize the service
Control first establishes what is safe: affected trains, blocked routes, available platforms, crew locations, and the next credible movement. Avoid promising a time before the signaller and operating lead agree on the plan. Station teams provide water, accessibility support, and a calm explanation of what is known. Record passengers needing an assisted transfer, medical help, or a connection that cannot wait.

## Offer usable alternatives
Choose options that can actually be staffed and dispatched: a short turnback, an accepting route, a replacement bus, or an endorsed ticket arrangement. Give passengers the action, destination, departure point, and next update time. Keep the message consistent across announcements, displays, web updates, and staff briefings. Do not send people onto an alternative route until capacity, accessibility, and ticket acceptance are confirmed.

## Recover and learn
When trains resume, prioritize stranded passengers and publish the revised stopping pattern. Tell station teams when normal service is genuinely restored. Close the response with a count of cancellations, assisted passengers, substitute vehicles, and unresolved claims; capture passenger reports while details are fresh.
