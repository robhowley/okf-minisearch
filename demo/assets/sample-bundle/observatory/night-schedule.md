# Night Schedule

## Purpose
A night schedule turns competing science, maintenance, and outreach requests into an executable sequence. The duty astronomer owns the schedule after the afternoon handoff and may reorder it when conditions or instrument health change.

## Planning
Reserve the first 30 minutes for startup, alignment, and a focus check. Group targets by sky position to limit dome rotation and unnecessary slews. Place time-critical observations, such as an exoplanet ingress or a transient follow-up, ahead of flexible deep fields. Add a ten-minute buffer after every instrument change and a longer weather buffer near the horizon. Each block names a target, filter or grating, exposure plan, cadence, estimated duration, and required calibration.

## Handoff
Publish the schedule before sunset in the observatory workspace and print one copy for the control room. During the night, mark each block complete, skipped, or interrupted with a short reason. A replacement block must state what it displaces. At dawn, attach the final schedule to the data handoff so analysts can distinguish planned gaps from instrument failures.
