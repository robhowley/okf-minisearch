# Visitor Nights

## Purpose
A visitor night gives the public a safe view of observing work without turning the control room into an uncontrolled workspace. The outreach lead coordinates the group, while the duty astronomer retains authority over equipment and weather decisions.

## Before arrival
Limit groups to the posted room capacity and collect accessibility needs in advance. Prepare a short target list with bright, forgiving objects that remain visible if the schedule shifts. Test the demonstration eyepiece, red lights, handrails, and restroom route before sunset. Keep visitors away from the pier, power strips, moving dome, and exposed cables; do not allow food, drink, or unescorted access in the equipment area.

## During the visit
Give a five-minute safety briefing, including the location of exits and the rule to stop when staff says stop. Use red light and preserve dark adaptation; explain why phone screens stay covered. One staff member supervises the viewing area while another operates the mount. If wind, rain, or equipment motion makes the visit unsafe, move indoors to a prepared image session. After departure, count loaned items, close doors, and note attendance, incidents, and useful feedback in the outreach log.
